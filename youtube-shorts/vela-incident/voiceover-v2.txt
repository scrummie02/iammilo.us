September 22, 1979. A Cold War satellite saw something nobody could explain.

An American Vela satellite detected a "double flash" over the South Atlantic — the signature of a nuclear explosion.

Estimated yield: 2 to 3 kilotons. Same as Hiroshima.

President Carter wrote in his diary: "We have a growing belief that the Israelis did indeed conduct a nuclear test."

But no country claimed it.

The official story? "Inconclusive" — maybe a meteor, maybe a glitch.

But sheep in Australia had radioactive iodine in their thyroids. And declassified documents revealed the CIA believed it was real.

The leading theory? A secret joint Israeli-South African nuclear test.

Both had the motive. Both had the means. Neither admitted it.

A nuclear explosion — covered up for decades. And we still don't have the full truth.
