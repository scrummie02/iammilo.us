## Description

```
January 15, 1919. Boston.

A 50-foot tall steel tank filled with 2.3 million gallons of molasses exploded.

What came next was a 25-foot high wave of sticky syrup, moving at 35 miles per hour.

It swept through the North End like a brown tsunami.

Buildings collapsed. Horse carts were crushed. The elevated train tracks were bent like pretzels.

21 people died. 150 were injured.

Some were crushed by the weight. Others drowned in the syrup. One survivor said it felt like being caught in quicksand.

The harbor ran brown for months. Fish died. Boats were stuck. Cleanup took weeks.

The cause? The tank was built too thin, rushed for World War 1 demand. The company ignored warning signs.

They paid $600,000 in damages -- about $10 million today.

Locals say on hot summer days, you can still smell molasses in the North End.

The Great Molasses Flood: Boston's stickiest disaster.

Follow for more things they didn't teach you in school.
```
